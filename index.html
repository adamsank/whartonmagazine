TThis magazine could have been called “on Friction”. In a world where frictionless has become our modus operandi, there is much to be said for the slow, painful act of creativity. Of bringing things into existence and to fruition. Abrahamic religions would venture to say it is a G-dly act, the genesis of the world commanding us all to a tripartite creative act of thought, action, and valorization: “G-d said, let there be… and there was… and G-d saw that it was good.” Translating our thoughts into a reality to be shared is at the heart of most origin myths. ‘In the beginning there was only Wakonda, the Great Spirit and all things, plants, animals, and humans, were spirits in his mind.’ Tseitsinako ‘made thoughts and the names of things, and thought things into existence.’ Brahman ‘thought of the waters and they were.’

We may not all believe in faith but there is an underlying current of the divine in creation, or at least of something greater at play. We do not say “I invented a painting” but rather “I created a painting”. Is it that we leave a piece of our souls for others to find in what we have brought into the world? Is it in that ‘clear and ancient harmony’ that we call inspiration? ‘If I knew where the good songs came from, I would go there more often,’ said Leonard Cohen. David Lynch felt, too, he was locked outside of this greater, other room from which ‘beautiful gifts’ of ideas flipped through for him to catch like fish.

As we pore over financial statements and input marketing simulation exercises into Claude, we wanted to carve out a space for creation and specifically for fiction. Self-help books of recent years have encouraged introspection, encouraging us to optimize for painlessness. Fiction, however, begets extrospection. It is the force pushing us out to see as others see, live as others live, feel as others feel. We create and so we know ‘The World is not Conclusion’. More than making room, we wanted to value these creations, to see ‘that it was good.’ It is by friction that we strike fire and so we hope by our fiction to kindle yours.
